Research

Observations, data assimilation and climate prediction.

Land–atmosphere coupling

How does soil moisture shape the weather above it?

We use tower observations, satellite retrievals and models to trace how water and energy move between the land surface and the atmosphere.

Land data assimilation

How accurately can we estimate the water stored in soil?

We combine microwave satellite retrievals with land surface models, and use ground measurements to diagnose errors in estimates of soil moisture.

When can land conditions help us anticipate extremes?

We test how soil moisture, vegetation and snow influence subseasonal-to-seasonal forecasts of heatwaves and drought.

Above the rice fields of Boseong

On Korea’s south coast, a 307-metre weather tower stands among rice paddies. Our group used four years of observations (2016–2019) to trace how water moves from the soil into the air.

Soil moisture and evaporation

March–May

As the soil grew wetter, a larger share of surface energy went into evaporation.

Correlation r = 0.35

June–August

Evaporation accounted for a large share of surface energy, even as soil moisture varied.

Correlation r = 0.01

September–November

The link was strongest in autumn. Wetter soils were associated with a larger share of energy going into evaporation.

Correlation r = 0.59

December–February

Evaporative fraction was most sensitive to soil moisture in winter. A small change in soil moisture corresponded to the steepest change in the fitted relationship.

Correlation r = 0.51
